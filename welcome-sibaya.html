How it fits together

Four short answers. Open the ones you want.

🧭Follow the colour›
●
Each group has its own colour, chosen by that group. Wherever you see it — a card, a pill, a heading, a chart — you are looking at that group.
●
The region has its own colour too (OPR purple unless your committee picks another). It marks the records of everyone at Sun Padel Sibaya, across every group.
●
Charcoal is the whole register — every club and region together, one rating each.

That is all the wayfinding you need: a group's colour means that group, the region's colour means the region, charcoal means everyone.

🏘️Groups are where you play›

Sun Padel Sibaya is made of groups — a regular evening, a section of the club, a ladies' morning. Each group has its own board: its ladder, its court card, its tournaments and its own recent matches.

On the door the groups sit side by side; swipe across to see them and tap one to go in. Enter a score asks which group you played in, then takes the score. You can enter a score for any group you play in.

👤Your page, and your rating›

There is one page per member, and it is the same page wherever it is opened from — a records list, a partnership, a ladder. It shows your rating, your level, where you stand, Move by group, every partnership you have had, and every match with its sets.

Claim it: from the door tap My page, find your name, choose a 4-digit PIN. Then add a photo — it appears beside your name across the app, and only you can change it.

You have one rating wherever you play. It moves after every match by how the result compared with what the ratings expected: beat a stronger pair and it moves more; lose to one and it costs little.

📖The records›

The records on the door opens three lists: List all members (name, group, rating), Member profiles (tap a name), and Ratings and ladders (pair ratings, the ladders, form).

You can read them for Sun Padel Sibaya, or for the whole register, where every club and region sits together. Back always returns you to the page you came from.

How your rating moves

Written out plainly, because it is the first thing everyone asks. Nothing here is a secret: the same sum runs for every match, at every club.

1
You start somewhere sensible. Your club sets a starting rating when you join. From then on it is yours — it follows you between groups and it is the only rating you have.
2
Before a ball is hit, the ratings expect a result. The two pairs' ratings say how the match “should” go. Beat a pair you were expected to lose to and your rating moves a lot; beat one you were expected to beat and it moves a little.
3
The margin counts, not just the win. Sets carry most of it and games the rest, so 6-1 6-2 moves more than 7-5 in a third. Lose narrowly to a strong pair and it costs you very little.
4
A longer match counts for more. Three sets move a rating more than one, with a sensible ceiling so no single night can run away with it.
5
A pair's rating is built from its two players — the stronger counts 60%, the other 40%. That is why a new pairing has a fair rating from its very first match, and why your own rating is what actually moves afterwards: both partners take the same movement.
An example. Two pairs are close on paper. You win 6-4 3-6 6-3 — a fair margin against an even opponent, so a modest move. Had the same result come against a much stronger pair, it would move a good deal more, and your opponents would lose the same amount you gained.

Two things follow that surprise people: you cannot lose much by playing someone far stronger, so play up whenever you can; and a rating is not a prize — it is a description of how you have been playing lately, and it changes every time you play.
