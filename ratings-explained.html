Your rating, and how it moves

Where your number starts, what a Level means, why a close loss costs less — and how one rating works across a whole club.

“Your rating is your reputation — and every match either backs it up or dents it.”

The rating scores how you played — sets, games, who you played, and how it compared to what was expected — not just who won.

2 · Two ways to start a club

A

The simple route — one default for everyone

Men 1500, ladies 1300. Start everyone there and let them play. Within a handful of matches the numbers separate and settle around each player's true level — someone who started too high loses rating to those beating them, someone too low climbs just as fast. This is how most clubs begin.

B

The bespoke route — grade your own players

If the club knows its players, the coach or committee places each one in a band from the table above and the whole list is pasted in at once. The very first ladder then already looks sensible; the engine fine-tunes from there.

Either way, you can't get it “wrong”. A starting rating is a starting point, not a verdict — the matches do the real grading.

Part two

4 · Four things decide how much it moves

1

Who you played

Beat someone the club expects you to beat and you barely move — you did what everyone knew you'd do. Beat someone stronger and you jump. That's news.

2

How convincingly

Squeaking through 7–5 in a decider says “it was tight”. Winning 6–1, 6–2 says “you were clearly better”. The system watches the games, not just who won the sets.

3

How much you played

A three-set battle carries more weight than a quick two-setter — there is more evidence in it. One set counts least, three sets most.

4

How close it was to expected

Before every match the system already expects a result — it knows the gap between the teams. Better than expected moves you up more; worse than expected costs you more. A brave loss never costs as much as a thrashing.

5 · A pair's strength

For two players of similar strength the pair is the blend of both. Where one partner is clearly stronger, that partner carries a little more of the weight — 60/40 — because that's how the game plays out on court. It's the same rule for Men's, Ladies, Mixed and All Sorts, and the pair's number is always the live blend of its two members' current ratings.

Part three

6 · Close matches — the three rules

1

Win the way you were expected to → full credit

Favourite, won by the margin predicted or better: full gain for you, full loss for them. A clean, convincing win always counts in full.

2

Scrape home by less than predicted → the loser is cushioned

If the underdog kept the games close, the loser's penalty slides down — the closer the games, the smaller the drop, to a minimum of about 10%. A brave loss always stings a little, never much.

3

Pull off an upset → full reward, no cushion

If the underdog actually wins, that's the most important result of all. Full reward for the winner, full loss for the favourite. Upsets are never softened.

Part four

8 · One rating, many groups

Your club on OPR is one register. You are in it once, with one padel rating. The club has groups — the Tuesday crowd, the ladies, the ballies — and you can play in any of them.

The club

Open to every member. Play here any time, with anyone.

Groups

Each has its own colour, pairs, ladder, tournaments and court cards. You join one by playing a match there. A group ranks you among its members — it never changes your rating.

Club ledger

Everyone across the club, one rating each, with every match you've played anywhere in the club.

So a match at the ladies' group and a match at the club move the same number. On a group's board you'll see your movement broken down by group — this group first — and the ledger shows the whole story.

Part five

9 · The questions everyone asks

“Why did my partner and I move differently? We played the same match!”

Because you didn't play the same story. Your ratings were different going in, so the club expected different things from each of you. The underdog who won gained more — more to prove, and proved it. The favourite gained less: winning was expected.

“Why am I still behind someone who keeps losing?”

Because they started from a higher reputation. Losing brings them down, but a reputation built over forty matches doesn't collapse in three. Keep winning and you will pass them — the system is slow to be fooled, in both directions.

“Does a close loss ever earn me points?”

No. A loss is always a loss — it just hurts less when you kept it close against a stronger team. You only ever climb by winning.

“I won in straight sets but only just — why did I gain less?”

The system watches games, not only sets. Expected to win comfortably but the games stayed tight — you didn't quite meet expectation, so the swing is smaller. Win more convincingly and you'll get the full gain.

“Why is a close loss between two even teams still a full penalty?”

The cushion rewards underdogs beating expectation. Between equal teams there is no underdog — it was a genuine 50/50 and it went against you. That's real news, so it counts in full.

“Someone has played more and won more sets than me — why is their rating lower?”

Wins are counted, but they aren't all weighed the same. A pile of wins against weaker pairs moves you less than a handful against strong ones. Open their profile in the app and look at who they actually played — the answer is usually right there.

“I play in two groups — do I have two ratings?”

No, one. Both groups rank the same number. What differs is where you stand among each group's members.

If you remember one thing

Beat who you're supposed to beat, you tick along.
Beat someone you shouldn't, you jump.
Push a stronger team close, your loss is cushioned.
Get thrashed by someone weaker, you drop.
The rating just keeps an honest record of what actually happened.

Part six

10 · Comparing with Playtomic (and similar apps)

Many players know their Playtomic level and want to know where they sit on OPR. The two systems measure differently — OPR's scale is wider and doubles-first — but as a practical rule of thumb:

OPR Level ≈ Playtomic level + 5.5 to 6

A Playtomic 4.0 typically sits around OPR Level 9.5–10; an OPR Level 7.5 player is broadly a Playtomic 1.5–2. A guide, not a conversion — no two rating systems map exactly.
