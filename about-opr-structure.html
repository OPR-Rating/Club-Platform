One engine. Three shapes of club.

A single club with its own app, a club with groups inside it, or an organisation running several sports — the same rating engine and the same app, arranged three ways. This page explains which is which.

OPR rates people, not accounts. A member is registered once, carries one rating per sport, and is ranked separately wherever they play. Everything below is a different answer to one question: who shares a register?

Rating lives with the person. Ranking lives with the place.

The three categories

Category 1

Standalone club

One club, one sport, its own app and its own database. Eswatini · Kloof Padel Ballies · Southbroom · Sun Fun Padel · WPA · Mamba · Sun Padel Sibaya

The original OPR products — OPR for padel, OTR tennis, OSR squash, OBR bowls. Each club is a world of its own: its members, its ladders, its tournaments, its ratings. Nothing is shared with anyone else, and nothing outside the club can touch it.

Category 2

Club with groups — one sport

A club running one discipline, with a master club at its centre and the groups that grow out of it. Club Alpha (the rehearsal) · Sun Padel Sibaya when it moves across

The club itself is the front door: open to every member, any time, with anyone. Beneath it sit the groups — the Tuesday ladies, the ballies, the social crowd — each with its own colour, code, pairs, ladder, tournaments and court cards, run by its own organiser exactly as if it were a standalone club. And behind them all, the club ledger: everyone, one rating each, every match from every group.

Category 3

Organisation — several sports

A club running more than one discipline under one membership. Project Vantage: padel, tennis, squash

One register of people for the whole organisation. Each sport switched on gets its own discipline pool — a category-2 club in its own right: master club, groups, ledger — in that sport's colours (OPR purple, OTR green, OSR orange). A member who plays two sports has two ratings, one per sport, and one login to nothing: no accounts, just their name.

What never changes

Across all three

The same rating engine, match for match. Scores entered by picking names — no PINs for players. Groups and clubs rank people; only playing moves a rating. A code opens the place it belongs to and nothing else. And a club can move from one shape to the next without moving its data: a standalone becomes a club with groups by adding a master club; a club becomes an organisation by switching on a second sport.
